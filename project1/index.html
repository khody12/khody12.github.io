Project Overview

This project involved restoring early 20th-century color photographs from the Russian Empire. The images were originally captured by Sergei Mikhailovich Prokudin-Gorskii as three separate black-and-white glass plate negatives using red, green, and blue filters. He won the Tzar's special permission to travel across the empire and take color photographs of everything he saw.

His idea was to record three exposures of every scene onto a glass plate using a red, a green, and a blue filter. Even though there was no way to print color photographs until much later.

By digitally overlaying and aligning these three channels, the original color and breathtaking beauty of the scenes can be restored, offering a vivid glimpse into a world over a century old.

Naive alignment

The naive solution to this problem is to just take each each image, make sure it's the same shape, and then layer the channels over each other. But as we can see, this doesn't look quite right.

This is because Sergei couldn't keep the camera absolutely perfectly steady as he changed the filters. Considering this was the early 1900s some small movements are to be expected. So there are some alignment issues that will need to be rectified.

Single-Scale Alignment

A way to align the color channels is to have a base channel, in my case blue, and compare it with the red and green channel and try to minimize the difference between the two images by comparing the pixels in some range like [-15, 15].

This would be accomplished by keeping one channel steady while shifting the other channel and measuring the sum of the distance between their values using NCC or L2 norm.

Essentially we would try shifting the red/green channel say 5px right, 5px up. If this has a lower displacement (i.e. the values are closer), then they should be more aligned. I used the L2 norm to accomplish this.
