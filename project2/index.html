Intro

In this project, I'm going to explore the power of manipulating the frequencies of an image to do blurring, sharpening, hybridization, and blending. We'll see how frequencies can be used for edge detection and how increasing the influence of edges can lead to a sharper image.

Convolution

The heart of working with filters is convolution.

Convolution is the process of overlaying a kernel (matrix) over another matrix and multiplying the corresponding overlaid values together. Then adding the sum of all these multiplications together, and setting the pixel of the resultant matrix to that sum. Once you overlay the kernel over each pixel of the original image, and get all the sums, you now have the result of convolution.

Here is an implementation of convolution in Python with a slower, per pixel approach, and a vectorized Numpy approach.

The four loop approach operates on each pixel, this is a very intuitive approach to the process but it ends up taking a long time, especially for large pictures or kernels. The vectorized NumPy approach operates on an entire vector at once.

Boundaries where a kernel would need to access parts of the image that don't exist are handled by padding the image with zeros on the border. It is padded by (height - 1) / 2 and (width - 1) / 2 on the top/bottom and the left/right respectively. Where height and width are the height and width of the kernel.

Creating a Binarized Image

In order to make a clear edge image, we can take the magnitude of the X and Y edges and then binarize the image. We force the pixels to be either 0 or 1 depending on their intensity. This removes noise and ensures that our edge map captures only the important edges.

I made the trade off of decreasing the necessary brightness to display an edge in order to make sure the camera man is clearly outlined. However, this has the side effect of bleeding some of the sharpest bits of background into the edge map. If, instead, you want less noise from the background but potentially a less clear camera man. You can increase the threshold for how intense an edge needs to be to be included in the binarized image.

Using a Gaussian Filter

The previous image had significant noise around the edges and the background had a noticeable amount of bleed in.

A way to counter these high frequencies that bleed in is using a Gaussian filter to blur out some of the noise and make sure that only the steepest and important edges are included in the final image.

First we blur the original image with a Gaussian filter. Then we proceed with using the differential operators, creating the magnitude image, and binarizing the magnitude image.

The result is significantly more defined and larger edges. There are fewer holes on the edges of the camera man and it generally results in a cleaner edge image. With that said, there are some trade offs. There is certainly some detail lost, specifically in the camera mans face. This can, of course, be adjusted by changing the threshold of displaying an edge, but it can result in more background bleed. Here I have tried to limit background bleed while capturing the largest and most prominent edges of the camera man.

Unsharp Mask Filter

We can use Gaussian smoothing to our advantage for more than just blurring. We can use it to sharpen an image by first creating a blurred image by convolving the original image with the Gaussian kernel and then subtracting the blurred image from our original image.

This leaves us with just the high frequencies of the image. From here, we can add these high frequencies back to the original image to try to accentuate the edges.

We can multiply this high frequency image by whatever sigma value we want. Perhaps 1.25 for some mild sharpening, or 3+ if we really want to see the edges.

Hybrid images

Now we're going to look at hybrid images. The idea here is that you can basically create two images in one by moving one image through a low pass filter and the other through a high pass filter.

The low pass image will cut out those high frequencies. While the high pass image will cut out the low frequencies. The image that goes through the low pass filter will be more visible at distance because the image that is composed of mostly high frequencies will not be visible by humans anymore. Our eye will not be able to see those high frequencies at a certain length.

Here's an example of a cat and a human.

The cat was run through the low pass filter, preserving those low frequencies so it will be seen from far away. While the human was run through a high pass filter, losing those low frequencies and making him less visible at a distance.

Smoothly Blending Images (Process)

The images above illustrate how the image stack works. We create some amount of levels, I chose 5, to be our image stack height.

Our goal is to first make a Gaussian stack, where each image is progressively blurrier. Then we'll create a Laplacian stack from our Gaussian stack. A Laplacian stack will contain bandpass images. Basically a certain range of frequencies. We get the Laplacian from the Gaussian by substracting the Gaussians levels.

For example, to get Laplacian stack level 0. We take the original image and the original image convolved with a Gaussian filter. We subtract the blurred image from the original, and this results in a details image of the highest frequencies. We continue this process until we don't have any further levels in the Gaussian stack to utilize.

This results in a Laplacian stack that has images containing certain bands of frequencies.

We will then utilize a mask. In this case, just a black and white square, black on one half, white on the other. Black will have a pixel value of 0, and white a pixel value of 1. We will construct our blended image by also putting our mask through a Gaussian stack to make it blurry and ease the transition between the white and black half.

$$ Hybrid\_Level = G_{mask} \cdot L_{apple} + (1 - G_{mask}) \cdot L_{orange} $$

We go through each level in the stack, create a stack for the hybrid image, and at the very end we will add up all of the images in the hybrid stack and that will be our final image.
